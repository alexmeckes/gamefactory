# Gameplay Gauntlet baseline

This is the durable starting brief for the gameplay redesign. It records human feedback and accepted technical evidence without treating either as more than it is.

## What revision 18 established

- The Unity 6 project imports and compiles.
- Factory-owned Input System replay can drive the visible player and crop.
- The crop exposes observable grip, pull, extraction, condition, acknowledgement, and continuation state.
- No-input quiescence and candidate-bound capture can support honest causal regression checks.

These are technical baseline facts. They are not evidence that the game is understandable, satisfying, deep, attractive, or fun.

## What direct human play rejected

- The controls did not make sense on first contact.
- The prototype put too much explanatory and telemetry-like text on screen.
- The slice felt designed around an automation key sequence rather than a natural player action.
- The evidence proved state transitions more strongly than prediction, physical correction, meaningful counterplay, or desire to continue.
- The delivered experience was substantially shallower than the intended iterative, specialist-reviewed Gauntlet process.

## Design obligation for the next revision

The next candidate must be a complete 60-to-90-second harvest encounter that a human can approach without knowing the scenario script. It must ask the player to read the world, choose a risk, execute and correct a physical pull, understand the result in-world, and immediately apply that learning to another decision. Trusted automation remains necessary for causality and regression safety; human play owns controls, comprehension, game feel, and fun.
