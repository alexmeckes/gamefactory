# Alchemy Delivery Boys — fresh product boundary

The authoritative concept is preserved verbatim in `concept.md`. Everything below is an explicit starting assumption delegated to the core-game director, not a frozen implementation recipe.

## Product promise

The player should feel like a small person doing alchemy in a living place: make something, carry it through the village, improvise when the world interferes, and hand the resulting potion to someone who visibly cares about what arrived.

## Assumptions for the first run

- Godot desktop game with direct controls and a compact, navigable world.
- A complete delivery should take minutes, not require waiting or offline progress.
- Brewing and travel must affect the same persistent potion state.
- The opening must contain movement, animation, feedback, and a consequential choice before exposition becomes dense.
- The two delivery boys should matter mechanically or expressively; they cannot be interchangeable menu portraits.
- The first slice proves one excellent delivery rather than a large recipe catalogue or village simulation.

## Hard exclusions

- No Oregon Trail-style sequence of mostly static illustrated panels.
- No recipe-selection menu followed by automatic travel.
- No hard-coded evidence counters, simulated fun claims, or concept art presented as a running game.
- No production asset batch before the core interaction survives real-engine evidence.

## Delegated decisions

Camera, exact controls, potion model, use of one or both boys, village topology, and the first villager problem remain open. The spec owner should commit to the smallest coherent solution and record falsifiers rather than enumerating options indefinitely.
