# Knot Theory — vertical-slice brief

## Hook

Every level is a single piece of string, and you are the tangle. Pull, thread,
loop, and pin a rope in 2D to match structures, free snagged objects, or bind
things together. Later puzzles should suggest knots as machines: pulleys,
trusses, and snares.

The design model is one toy explored exhaustively: the density and surprise of
Öoo, crossed with the practical clarity of a sailor's handbook.

## What this build must prove

1. Moving a rope directly can feel responsive and understandable.
2. A player can learn at least three verbs without a conventional tutorial.
3. The same rope representation can support several distinct goal types.
4. Success is based on meaningful spatial/topological state rather than tracing
   an arbitrary silhouette pixel-perfectly.
5. A five-to-ten-minute sequence can end on a recombination puzzle that feels
   like a small machine.

## Product boundaries

- Desktop, mouse-first, 1152×720.
- One local player, no accounts, no network, no procedural monetization loop.
- Code-native visuals and audio are acceptable and preferred for this slice.
- Keep the project small enough for one agent to understand and repair.

## Open design questions

- Whether topology is represented through winding, crossings, tension, ordered
  contacts, or a hybrid.
- Whether pins are placed freely or live at authored anchors.
- The exact teaching sequence and number of puzzles.
- How literal the workshop/sailor presentation should be.
- Which advanced machine idea—pulley, truss, or snare—makes the best finale.

## Honest success criteria

The automated run should verify importability, stable rope simulation, multiple
interaction verbs, progression, and at least one solved goal. A human still has
to judge tactility, clarity, delight, and whether the finale earns its surprise.
